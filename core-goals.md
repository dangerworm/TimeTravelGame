# Core Goals — The Spine

_This is the north star. Every mechanic must serve something on this page, or it gets cut._
_Established in the redesign conversation of 5 June 2026. See `archive/` for the prior design._

---

## What kind of game this is

A **competitive expedition / engine-building game** for rival scientists who co-discovered time
travel. You build a team and a machine, then use them to **solve the challenge each destination
presents**, racing to the greatest scientific legacy — while the timeline you all share quietly
remembers how greedy everyone has been.

Touchstones to study and steal from: **Lost Ruins of Arnak** (build a toolkit, overcome expedition
obstacles, climb a track) and **Clank!** (push-your-luck on a shared board with a collective threat
that greed summons).

---

## The spine (the one decision players make over and over)

> **"How do I crack this destination — and how hard do I dare push?"**

Each jump is a small **expedition with obstacles**. The obstacles can be solved in different ways,
and **your team is the toolkit that decides which approaches are open to you**. You commit a plan,
something is revealed, and you adapt.

- **Dominant feel: tactical puzzle.** Information is largely visible; you out-think the destination
  with the tools you built. This is the part Drew most enjoys — navigating and solving challenges.
- **Optional layer: the gamble.** You can always choose to _push_ — overclock, dig deeper — for more
  reward at escalating, heart-in-mouth risk.

If this core choice is not fun round after round, nothing else matters. Prototype it first.

---

## The four emotional keystones (non-negotiable)

These are the feelings the game must deliver. They came straight from Drew and outrank any
individual mechanic.

1. **The overclock thrill.** Push the boundary; it could go either way. Push-your-luck must survive
   into the final design in some form.
2. **Team legacy.** The deep satisfaction of bringing together brilliant minds who build on _your_
   discovery and make it greater. The team is an engine that grows and opens new possibilities. Keep
   the "a veteran retires and leaves a permanent legacy" payoff beat.
3. **Collapse is possible, but never a dead stop.** The shared timeline _can_ fail — but it is never
   a Pandemic-style buzzer that erases everyone's effort two turns from glory. Collapse is a
   dramatic **third-act mode-flip**, not game-over (see below).
4. **The spine is challenge-navigation, not ethics.** "How you treat history" is _one pillar of
   several_, not the heart of the game.

---

## Two laws that govern every mechanic (added 5 Jun 2026)

These sit above any individual mechanic. A rule that breaks one of these is the rule that's
wrong.

- **Narrative coherence.** Every mechanic must be explicable in the fiction. If we can't say _why_
  the machine/team/timeline behaves this way, players can't emotionally invest, and it doesn't go
  in. Mechanics and story are co-designed.
- **Accessible to the heart, not just the head.** The game must be a great night for someone who
  comes for the roleplay and the feeling, not the optimisation — nobody should ever feel stupid.
  The surface decision stays intuitive and thematic; tactical depth is opt-in, layered on top.
  The gamble is the leveller (anyone can have a heroic or disastrous moment); failure always
  reads as drama, never as a maths error exposed to the table.

---

## How the pieces hang together

- **Engine-building team.** Recruit specialists; each opens new ways to solve problems and new eras
  to reach. Growth = more tools = harder challenges accessible = bigger rewards.
- **Push-your-luck overclock.** A clean "push it" option on a jump: more reward, more risk, a cost
  paid in instability. One instability track, not two.
- **Shared Timeline Integrity.** Aggressive approaches (smash-and-grab, overclock failures,
  interference) shave a shared integrity track. As it drops, _everyone's_ jumps get harder — which
  feeds the spine (more challenge) rather than being a flat penalty.
- **Ethics as the dial, not the spine.** The ethical axis is what _fuels_ the collapse threat: if
  the table is collectively greedy, integrity slides toward collapse; if everyone is careful, it
  never does. Emergent, player-driven — aim for it threatening **at most ~50% of games**.
- **Collapse as mode-flip.** Cross the brink and the game _transforms_ rather than ending: the
  closed loop is failing, so the only way to preserve your legacy is to escape into the Many Worlds
  before reality unravels. An uneasy scramble — keep the timeline limping while you each race your
  own escape. Stakes and alliances change; nobody's effort is erased.
- **Multiple visible scoring axes.** No player should realise on round 3 that they cannot win.
  Several legible paths to legacy, a telegraphed endgame everyone can see approaching, and ideally a
  two-axis legacy (what you discovered × how you did it) so different playstyles are all viable.

## What "success" means for this project

- ~80%: **Drew genuinely loves playing it** with his husband and 2–4 friends. Enjoyment and wow
  factor above all.
- ~20%: the legitimate ego reward of **"_You_ made this? That's awesome."**

Design for a table of **4–5 players**. Treat larger/smaller counts as variants to tune later.

## Salvaged from the old design (the content bible)

These survive the rebuild and feed the new skeleton:

- The fiction / foreword — the co-discovery premise.
- **Real historical mysteries** as destination content; reward scales with how genuinely unknown the
  subject is (the mystery tiers). This is the game's most distinctive, marketable hook.
- The sell-vs-publish dilemma (cash now vs. respect) — folds into the approach choices.
- Machine stages as a gate on which eras you can reach (simplified).
- Team retirement leaving a permanent legacy.

## Parked for v1 (darlings to re-earn later, not delete)

Age-track micro-bookkeeping, two instability types, three combo-gated specials,
modules-with-sub-rules, field training, dismissal, the full three-dice system. Add any of these back
only when a playtest proves the game needs it.
