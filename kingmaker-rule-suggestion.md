# Kingmaker rule — a suggestion to react to

_Written overnight 11 Jun 2026 for Drew to review. **This is a proposal, not a decision.** It's the
one genuinely-open design thread (GDD §14 / `tasks.md`): the Many Worlds alliance is the intended
endgame, but the alliance-ending **mechanics** aren't specified — who makes the winning jump, and
how is the payoff shared so the ending is **neither a foregone conclusion nor a kingmaker** handing
one player the game. Read it gut-first; push against it._

---

## The problem, stated plainly

Many Worlds is a 5-step × 5-pip gauntlet (way past one lab's strength), so it's built to be
attempted by a **full-table alliance** pooling researchers. That creates three failure modes the
rule has to dodge **all** of:

1. **Foregone conclusion** — only one player can realistically reach/trigger MW, so they win by
   fiat.
2. **Kingmaker** — a trailing player who can't win decides _who else_ wins by choosing whom to lend
   to.
3. **Winner-take-the-lump** — whoever makes the final jump vacuums a huge reputation prize (the
   printed ~11) and wins regardless of the game that came before, so lending to them is just gifting
   the game.

And it has to dodge them **without breaking the two locked laws**: _Reputation only ever traces to
your own researcher's work_ (never shared/transferred), and _rental is Cash-only_.

---

## The proposal: an **open, co-authored** Many Worlds jump

Three moves, each aimed at one failure mode.

### 1. The jump is _declared_, then _open_ — not a private handoff

On the turn of any player who has reached **Amp 7** (the only ones who can open the door), they may
**declare a Many Worlds attempt**. Once declared, **every player may commit researchers to it before
it resolves** — nobody can be frozen out, and nobody is the sole gatekeeper. The declarer is just
the host whose machine opens the door; hosting carries **no scoring bonus of its own.**

> _Kills the kingmaker (#2):_ because contribution is open to all, no single player controls who
> benefits from the jump. There is no one to bribe and no one to freeze out — so no one holds the
> casting vote over someone else's win.

### 2. Cash is negotiated; **Reputation is automatic, by contribution**

- The **Cash** layer stays exactly as the game already works: lending a researcher to the jump is a
  **Negotiate rental** at a freely-agreed Cash fee (Cash-only, no loans — unchanged). This is where
  the table-talk, the bluffing, and the deal-making live.
- The **Reputation** payoff is **not** negotiated and **not** a lump to the host. On success, the
  multiverse opening pays a **co-author credit**: **+1 Reputation to a player for each of their own
  researchers that was committed to the winning jump** (provisional cap **+5**). Every contributor —
  host included, on equal terms — is credited only for the work _their own_ researchers did.

> _Keeps the two laws:_ each player's rep still comes from _their own_ researchers doing the work
> (here, the most important work humanity ever does). Nothing is transferred or shared. Rental stays
> Cash-only.
>
> _Kills the winner-take-the-lump (#3):_ there is no 11-point lump. The door's glory is **divided by
> who actually pushed on it**, so making the final jump is not, by itself, worth points.

### 3. The trailing players have the most to gain by going all-in

Because co-author rep scales with **how many of your researchers you throw at the door**, the
players who are **behind** have every reason to **commit their whole team** to the winning jump —
and earn the biggest co-author credit at the table — while the leader, ahead on banked reputation,
must decide whether to expose their team too (and earn the credit) or hold back (and let the
trailing labs close the gap on the climactic jump).

> _Kills the foregone conclusion (#1):_ the leader can't just coast to the door. If they don't
> contribute, the alliance can still open it **without** them and the trailing contributors bank
> co-author rep the leader doesn't — a real, earned catch-up the leader can only neutralise by
> committing their own team to the shared risk. The ending becomes a **final scramble of nerve**,
> not a coronation. (And it's not a patronising catch-up band-aid — the trailing players _earn_ it
> by taking the biggest gamble at the most dangerous moment.)

## How a Many Worlds attempt resolves (concrete)

1. A player at **Amp 7** declares the attempt on their turn.
2. **Any player may commit researchers** (Capacity is ignored for MW — everyone for the final push,
   per the sim's working assumption). Cash rental fees are settled by free negotiation.
3. Build one combined skill deck from all committed researchers' pips (+ each contributor's traces?
   — **open Q below**). Resolve the **5 steps × 5 pips** gauntlet with the normal hand/overclock
   rules.
4. **All five steps cleared → Triumph.** The game ends. Award co-author Reputation (+1 per committed
   researcher per owner, cap +5), then everyone tallies the normal two-band score
   (`Rep − Disrepute + highest module level + unresearched artefacts`). **Highest total wins.**
5. **Any step fails → −2 Timeline Integrity** (the gauntlet frays the timeline), the attempt ends,
   researchers return to their owners. The alliance may try again later — if the timeline survives
   it.

## Open sub-questions for you (gut answers welcome)

- **Co-author currency: researcher _count_ or _pips_?** I picked **count** (legible, near-zero
  arithmetic — fits the "minimal sums" law). **Pips** is more honest (a 15-pip expert > a 3-pip
  junior) but adds a sum and invites min-maxing the count. Count can be swarm-gamed with cheap
  juniors; the **+5 cap** is the guard. Your call.
- **The +5 cap and the +1 rate** are pure guesses — untuned by the sim (the sim has no alliance).
  The printed MW rep was ~11; a 3–4-way split lands each contributor around +3–5, which feels right
  against era-deep papers worth 8–10. Watch at the table.
- **Whose traces pollute the combined deck?** Cleanest for a first playtest: **only the host's**
  instability/traces carry in (it's their machine opening). Alternative: everyone's, which makes a
  clean-machine ally genuinely valuable to recruit. Lean: host-only for now.
- **Does the host need a _find_/objective at all?** I built the MW cards with **no find step** — the
  door is the whole prize. Confirm that's the feel you want (no consolation cash).
- **Two or more players at Amp 7 racing:** fine as-is — each may declare on their own turn; the
  first _successful_ declaration ends it. No extra rule needed, I think.

## If you hate the open version — the simpler fallback

**"Co-discovery" variant:** keep it a _closed_ alliance (the declarer privately recruits lenders via
Cash rental as today), but still pay the **co-author rep by contribution** rather than a lump to the
host. This preserves the cosy back-room-deal feel of an alliance while still refusing to hand any
one player a decisive prize. It reopens a _softer_ kingmaker (the declarer chooses who to invite)
but defangs the sharp one (no one wins the game just by being invited). It's less elegant but closer
to the "full-table alliance" image in your notes. I prefer the **open** version — it's the only one
that kills all three failure modes — but this is the safe retreat if open feels too cold.

---

_My honest read: the load-bearing idea is **"co-author rep by contribution, host gets no bonus."**
That single move converts the ending from "who gets handed the lump" into "who dares commit hardest
to the last jump," which is exactly the team-legacy keystone firing one final time — the
co-discovery fiction you opened the game with, paid off at the end. The open-vs-closed choice is
secondary; decide that on feel._
