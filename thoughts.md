# Thoughts

These are things that have occurred to me but which I haven't fully fleshed out, or which I'm not
sure add much/anything to the game, or might make it overly complicated.

## Many Worlds as intermediate unlock (Option B)

Currently Many Worlds triggers end game immediately (Option A). An alternative: Many Worlds becomes
a significant mid-game unlock rather than the finishing move. Benefits would include insulation from
closed loop consequences, access to new jump possibilities, and a legacy point bonus for having
achieved it. End game would instead be triggered by something else - a fixed round count, a points
threshold, or a specific final achievement.

Downside: loses the dramatic "player X ends the game on their terms" moment that Option A provides.
Option A kept for now; revisit if the end game feels anticlimactic in playtesting.

## Failure

Doing anything in the past is inherently risky. Perhaps you send a drone to the middle ages and
inadvertently create a new religion, or mess up scientific advancement (faster or slower) for the
future. Perhaps you cause a discovery to be made years ahead of its time, which means the great
minds like Da Vinci or Newton are ignored because it was discovered years before and never become
famous, or they led a totally different life because their area of interest was already completely
researched, thus causing humanity to fall backwards because other key discoveries they made never
came to pass.

Risks of travelling could be decided by a dice roll - more dice for more risk - but mitigated by
certain technologies or team members. e.g. a reliability engineer might reduce the number of dice
rolled by 1. Dice could be six-sided with 0, 1, or 2 hazards possible. Travel cards might cause the
player to have to roll 0-2 dice.

_Resolved: typed dice system - Die 1 (mild) and Die 2 (severe). Destination card specifies 0/1/2
dice. Outcomes are typed (missed opportunity, comms failure, machine failure) with matching typed
mitigation via capability table stars. Machine failure consequence specified per destination card.
Overclock forces Die 2 regardless of destination risk._

## Action handling _(resolved)_

Player has 3 general actions per turn usable on anything. Each team member can take at most 1 action
per turn (exhausts), limited to what their capability table allows (Red = blocked, Green = standard,
Star = bonus). The 5 phases are: React, Jump, Realise (sell/write papers), Develop, Plan. Late game,
if the team covers all routine actions, the player can spend all 3 general actions on a single
3-action special (candidates: Breakthrough, Landmark Paper, Temporal Anchor).

Worried you won't get the best price for an artefact? A cultural specialist might know someone in
the field who will pay more.

## Freedom in negotiation _(agreed)_

Players can haggle to get whatever they want from a deal to use a time machine. Did the player
forget to ask for anything in return? Their fault. Did they ask for too much? The other player can
refuse. Do several players have an offer to pair up? Take the best offer or only take two of the
three. No enforced transaction structure - all deals are freely negotiated at the table.

## Intentional abandonment / planted agent

Could a player deliberately leave a team member at a destination rather than as an accident? A
"planted agent" left in 1400 AD could theoretically gather intelligence across multiple turns -
returning with information on future jumps, or scouting a destination the player can't yet reach.

Very complex to model. What do they bring back? How does a player retrieve them? Does the agent
accumulate something each round? Probably too involved for now, but the thematic possibility is
interesting.

Related: the accidental stranding mechanic already establishes that team members can survive in the
past, so the fictional groundwork exists.

## Sabotage card

One very rare card per player, shuffled into the deck at setup. Allows you to disrupt an allied
player - steal some materials, cause a jump to fail, or introduce a hazard into their next roll.

The gains would need to be worthwhile, and the consequences of being sabotaged shouldn't cause
lasting damage. Playing it burns a once-per-game resource and poisons the alliance permanently - so
the threat of holding it may be more valuable than playing it (implicit leverage over an ally).

Risk: too powerful and alliances become unviable; too weak and nobody ever plays it. Hard to balance
because it only fires once per game per player. Might work better as a "threat" mechanic - the value
is in holding it, not using it. Possibly belongs in the Lending / Alliance section once that is more
developed.

## Instability vs unpredictability token _(resolved)_

Renamed to **instability token** throughout. Both sources (overclock and hasty react) are framed as
the operation becoming less stable. Document updated.

## Visitation (Stage 1) as peak risk

Stage 1 (Visitation) is the riskiest machine stage to operate - the portable return pad has a
one-hour limit and you can be stranded. Stage 2 (Extended Presence) is explicitly more reliable.
This means players experience peak personal risk in the middle of the machine development track, not
at the beginning.

This feels right: early game you observe safely via drone (Stage 0), then you brave in-person visits
with the risky portable pad (Stage 1), then you graduate to a more reliable mechanism (Stage 2).
The risk peak at Stage 1 encourages progression. Worth confirming this is the intended feel, and
that destination cards are written accordingly - Stage 1-accessible destinations should carry more
Die 1/Die 2 risk to reflect the fragile equipment.

## Artefact processing _(resolved)_

Artefact cards are held in hand after a jump - not automatically converted. Selling costs 1 action
(face value). Writing a paper costs 1 action (legacy points). Doing both in sequence costs 2 actions
(face value + small provenance bonus). Selling forecloses the paper; writing doesn't foreclose
selling. Holding multiple cards to write papers in one turn (via postdocs) is a valid late-game
strategy. Cultural specialist's star on Process Returns = bonus currency when selling.
