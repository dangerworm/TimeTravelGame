## Currently working on: **The core jump / expedition loop**

This is the spine (see `core-goals.md`) and the thing we must prototype first. Everything else is
downstream of this being fun.

### The question we're answering

Is choosing **how to crack a destination** — tactical-puzzle-dominant, with an optional gamble —
genuinely fun, round after round?

### Sketch so far (provisional — argue with it)

- A destination is a small expedition: a **discovery** sits behind one or more **obstacles**.
- Each obstacle can be tackled different ways. **Your team is the toolkit** that decides which
  approaches are open (e.g. historian → talk your way in; engineer → build a workaround; brute force
  always available but noisy + damaging to the timeline).
- You commit a plan, then a **reveal** (complication and/or dice) forces you to adapt — this is the
  "navigation."
- At any point you may **push / overclock** for more reward at escalating risk.
- Aggressive solutions shave the shared **Timeline Integrity** track.

### Open questions to resolve

- What exactly is an "obstacle"? How many per destination? How are approaches represented?
- What does the team "toolkit" look like as cards/tokens? How does a member _open_ an approach?
- What's the reveal/adaptation mechanism that keeps it from being solvable up front (so it stays a
  navigation, not a calculation)?
- Where does push-your-luck physically sit in the resolution?
- Minimum viable version to test on paper: how few obstacles/approaches/team cards can still produce
  an interesting decision?
